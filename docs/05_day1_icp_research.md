# Day 1 — ICP Research: US Commercial Cleaning

**Project:** RepFlow GHL — Commercial Cleaning GTM Engine  
**Date:** 2026-09-18  
**Status:** Working hypothesis; not yet a validated ICP

## Objective

Define a defensible first-pass ICP for RepFlow's client-owned GoHighLevel implementation and CRM-management offer before spending meaningful Clay credits.

The core question is not "which companies are cleaning companies?" It is:

> Which US commercial-cleaning operators have enough recurring B2B revenue, sales-process complexity, and observable CRM/follow-up friction to justify a professionally implemented and managed lead-to-contract system?

## Market evidence observed

Current cleaning-specific CRM products repeatedly market around the same commercial workflow: lead capture, walkthrough scheduling, quote/proposal management, follow-up, pipeline visibility, recurring contracts, and lead-source reporting. This is strong problem evidence for the workflow RepFlow intends to improve.

Current commercial-cleaning operators also commonly sell through a sequence of inquiry -> facility walkthrough -> written scope/proposal -> recurring service. This makes a CRM opportunity object and follow-up automation more relevant than a simple appointment-booking funnel.

## Initial ICP thesis

### Primary segment

Established, independently owned or regional **B2B commercial-cleaning / janitorial companies in the United States** that:

- primarily serve businesses/facilities rather than residential customers;
- sell recurring janitorial or facility-cleaning contracts;
- use a walkthrough / estimate / proposal sales motion;
- operate across a metro, multiple cities, or multiple locations;
- have enough lead volume or sales activity that missed follow-up is economically meaningful;
- are large enough to pay for implementation/management, but not so enterprise-scale that procurement and an entrenched RevOps stack make a small specialist vendor impractical;
- show signs of growth, market expansion, sales hiring, active digital acquisition, or sales-process complexity.

### Likely sweet spot

The working sweet spot is **local-to-regional operators** rather than:

- solo / very small residential cleaners; or
- large national franchise networks / enterprise facility-service companies.

Headcount will be treated as a proxy, not as the primary ICP definition. In cleaning, field-worker count can be high without corresponding sales complexity.

## Hard gates — V0

An account must pass all of these before paid enrichment:

1. United States based / serves a US market.
2. Commercial cleaning / janitorial is a primary service.
3. Recurring commercial service is offered or strongly implied.
4. Business has an active website and an identifiable company/domain.
5. There is a plausible business buyer/owner/sales decision maker.
6. Account is not obviously a huge national enterprise or pure marketplace.
7. Account is not primarily residential / maid service.

## Fit dimensions — V0 (no final weights yet)

### A. Commercial specialization
Signals:
- recurring janitorial;
- offices, medical, industrial, education, property management;
- commercial quote / walkthrough language;
- multi-site service.

### B. Business maturity
Signals:
- years in business;
- multiple service areas;
- client count / locations where publicly stated;
- established management team;
- certifications / insurance / procurement readiness;
- repeatable service process.

### C. Sales-process complexity
Signals:
- quote or consultation forms;
- on-site walkthrough before proposal;
- multiple services / verticals;
- multiple territories;
- dedicated sales / BD roles;
- proposals/RFPs;
- active outbound or lead-generation language.

### D. CRM opportunity
Signals:
- generic contact/quote forms with no visible scheduling workflow;
- response promised manually (e.g., "we will contact you");
- no obvious automated quote-follow-up journey;
- multiple lead sources / service areas likely to require routing;
- sales hiring or growth that increases pipeline-management complexity;
- current CRM is absent, unclear, or appears fragmented.

**Important:** absence of visible technology is not proof the company lacks a CRM. It is only a research flag until verified.

## Signal dimensions — V0

These should increase *timing priority*, not base fit:

- hiring for sales / business development;
- opening new territories or locations;
- franchise expansion;
- new service line or vertical expansion;
- visible increase in commercial marketing activity;
- new website / active growth initiative;
- multi-location/national-account expansion;
- relevant technology/job postings that imply CRM change or process investment.

## Negative / exclusion signals

- residential-first cleaning business;
- one-person / micro operator with minimal sales infrastructure;
- one-off cleaning only;
- no working website or business identity;
- very large national enterprise/franchise where buying process is likely centralized and complex;
- already demonstrates a sophisticated integrated sales/CRM stack with little apparent gap;
- poor data confidence.

## Decision-maker hypothesis

Priority personas for RepFlow acquisition:

1. Owner / Founder / President — smaller local operators.
2. General Manager / Operations leader — established local firms.
3. Director/VP of Sales or Business Development — regional firms.
4. Marketing / Growth leader — when digital lead generation is clearly owned there.
5. CRM / Systems / RevOps owner — uncommon in this segment but high relevance when present.

## First offer hypothesis

**Commercial Cleaning Lead-to-Contract System**

RepFlow implements the revenue layer inside client-controlled infrastructure:

lead capture -> rapid response -> qualification -> routing -> walkthrough/estimate -> proposal pipeline -> follow-up -> won/lost -> reactivation -> reporting.

RepFlow does **not** need to replace field-service scheduling, workforce, payroll, or cleaning-operations software. Those systems can be integrated where useful.

## What Day 2 must validate

- Whether commercial-only vs mixed commercial/residential materially predicts fit.
- Which maturity proxies best distinguish buyers from tiny operators.
- Whether multi-location/service-area complexity predicts CRM need.
- Which observable website patterns correlate with likely follow-up friction.
- Whether sales/BD hiring is common enough to use as a signal.
- Whether we should target one metro cluster first or remain nationwide.
- Which 3–5 fields are worth spending Clay credits on.

## Research discipline

No revenue, headcount, CRM ownership, lead volume, or automation maturity will be asserted unless supported by a reliable source. Unknown stays unknown. Manual labels are hypotheses until enriched or verified.
